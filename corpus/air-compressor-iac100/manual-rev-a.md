# Industrial Air Compressor IAC-100 — Maintenance & Operations Manual (Rev. A)

## Overview & Specifications

The IAC-100 is a belt-driven rotary screw air compressor supplying
pneumatic tools and the edge bander's hold-down system at Dawlia
Furniture Works' Damietta facility. Tank capacity: 500 liters. Maximum
working pressure: 10 bar. Drive: electric motor, belt-driven screw
airend. The system stores significant compressed-air energy at all
times when pressurized, which is the central hazard addressed throughout
this manual.

## Safety Prerequisites

1. Before any maintenance on the compressor, tank, or piping, isolate
   electrical power at the lockable disconnect and fully bleed tank
   pressure via the manual drain valve, confirming zero on the pressure
   gauge before opening any fitting.
2. Never disable or adjust the pressure relief valve setting — it is the
   last line of defense against tank over-pressurization.
3. Confirm the belt guard is in place and secured before starting the
   compressor; the belt and pulley are an entanglement hazard.
4. Do not perform maintenance near the auto-start compressor without
   first engaging the lockout at the disconnect switch — the compressor
   can start automatically on a pressure drop with no warning, even
   while apparently idle.
5. Check the tank's condensate drain and confirm it is functioning at
   least daily — accumulated condensate accelerates tank corrosion and
   can compromise the tank's pressure rating over time.
6. Wear hearing protection near the compressor during operation; ambient
   noise at the unit exceeds 85dB.

## Installation & Setup

Install the IAC-100 on a level pad with adequate ventilation clearance
per the manufacturer's airflow specification — the airend rejects
significant heat and inadequate ventilation causes premature thermal
shutdown. Connect three-phase power through a lockable disconnect
mounted within reach of the maintenance area. Pipe the discharge line
with a minimum slope back toward the tank to prevent condensate pooling
in the distribution piping. Commission the pressure relief valve with a
manufacturer-certified test before first use.

## Operating Procedures

Confirm the condensate drain valve is closed before starting. Start the
compressor and allow it to build to working pressure before opening any
downstream tool or hold-down circuit. Monitor the pressure gauge during
operation; a failure to reach or hold working pressure should be treated
as a fault, not worked around by running tools at reduced pressure.
Shut down via the control panel's normal stop sequence, not by cutting
power at the disconnect, except in an emergency.

## Maintenance Schedule

Daily: check and operate the condensate drain, visually inspect for air
leaks at fittings. Weekly: check belt tension and condition, check oil
level in the airend lubrication system. Monthly: inspect and clean
intake air filter, test pressure relief valve function per manufacturer
procedure. Every 2,000 operating hours: full airend service by a
certified technician, including oil and separator element change.

## Diagnostic & Troubleshooting

1. Symptom: compressor fails to reach working pressure. Likely cause: a
   significant air leak in the distribution piping, or a clogged intake
   air filter restricting airend performance. Corrective action:
   inspect distribution piping fittings for audible/soapy-water-
   detectable leaks, inspect and clean the intake filter per the
   monthly checklist before assuming an airend fault.
2. Symptom: excessive condensate accumulation, more than the daily drain
   cycle can handle. Likely cause: unusually high ambient humidity, or a
   failing automatic drain valve. Corrective action: increase manual
   drain frequency temporarily, inspect the automatic drain valve
   mechanism for sticking or blockage.
3. Symptom: compressor short-cycles (starts and stops far more
   frequently than normal). Likely cause: a downstream air leak causing
   rapid pressure drop, or a failing pressure switch. Corrective action:
   inspect for leaks per item 1 above before assuming a pressure switch
   fault; short-cycling increases wear and should not be left
   unaddressed.
4. Symptom: unusual belt noise or visible belt wear. Likely cause: belt
   tension out of specification, or pulley misalignment. Corrective
   action: isolate power and lock out per Safety Prerequisite §4 before
   inspecting; check belt tension against the weekly checklist
   specification.
