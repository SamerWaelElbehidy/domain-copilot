# Spray Finishing Booth SFB-300 — Maintenance & Operations Manual (Rev. A)

## Overview & Specifications

The SFB-300 is a downdraft spray finishing booth used to apply lacquer
and solvent-based coatings to furniture components. Booth dimensions:
6m x 4m x 2.8m. Airflow: downdraft extraction rated at 0.5 m/s face
velocity minimum. Filtration: intake pre-filter plus floor-grate exhaust
filters rated for lacquer overspray. All electrical fittings inside the
booth envelope are explosion-proof rated for the solvent vapor
classification in use.

## Safety Prerequisites

1. Confirm booth exhaust airflow reads at or above 0.5 m/s on the
   face-velocity gauge before starting any spray application — never
   spray with extraction below this threshold.
2. Verify no ignition sources (phones, non-rated tools, open flame) are
   present inside the booth before every use; only explosion-proof rated
   equipment may be powered inside the booth envelope.
3. Confirm the correct respirator cartridge (organic vapor, rated for
   the coating's solvent type per its safety data sheet) is fitted and
   within its service life before spraying.
4. Check that exhaust filters are not saturated (visual color-change
   indicator or differential-pressure gauge in the red zone) before
   starting a shift — saturated filters both reduce extraction and
   increase fire load.
5. Do not accumulate overspray/solvent-soaked rags inside the booth;
   dispose of them in the covered, fire-rated waste container
   immediately after use.
6. Confirm the booth's fire suppression nozzles are unobstructed and the
   system status light shows "ready" before starting production.

## Installation & Setup

Install the SFB-300 with its exhaust ducted directly outdoors, away from
any building air intake, per the ducting diagram in Appendix C. All
electrical work inside the booth envelope must be performed by a
technician certified for hazardous-location wiring; standard fittings
are not permitted inside the spray zone or exhaust plenum. Commission
face-velocity readings across the full booth width before first use and
record the baseline for future filter-saturation comparison.

## Operating Procedures

Confirm the coating's safety data sheet respirator cartridge requirement
matches the cartridge fitted before starting. Start booth extraction and
allow airflow to stabilize (minimum 30 seconds) before beginning spray
application. Apply coating in the pattern specified by the finish
schedule; do not exceed the recommended wet-film thickness per pass, as
excess buildup increases both fire load and cure-time. Allow flash-off
time between coats as specified before applying the next layer.

## Maintenance Schedule

Daily: check face-velocity gauge, visually inspect filters, empty the
fire-rated rag waste container. Weekly: clean floor grates, inspect
booth lighting fixtures for solvent residue buildup. Monthly: replace
exhaust filters per the saturation indicator or scheduled interval,
whichever comes first; test fire suppression system per manufacturer
procedure. Quarterly: certified inspection of all explosion-proof
electrical fittings.

## Diagnostic & Troubleshooting

1. Symptom: face-velocity gauge reads below the 0.5 m/s threshold.
   Likely cause: saturated exhaust filters, or a partially closed
   damper in the ducting. Corrective action: inspect and replace
   filters per Safety Prerequisite §4 before any further spraying;
   verify the ducting damper is fully open.
2. Symptom: visible haze or odor persists in the booth after the flash-
   off period. Likely cause: insufficient extraction, or coating applied
   above the recommended wet-film thickness. Corrective action: confirm
   face velocity is within spec, review the operator's applied film
   thickness against the finish schedule before the next panel.
3. Symptom: fire suppression system status light shows a fault, not
   "ready". Likely cause: low system pressure, or an obstructed nozzle
   detected by the system's self-check. Corrective action: do not spray
   until the fault is cleared by a certified technician; this is a hard
   stop, not a judgment call for the operator.
4. Symptom: uneven coating finish (orange peel or runs) unrelated to
   airflow. Likely cause: spray gun pressure/tip mismatch for the
   coating viscosity, or booth temperature/humidity outside the
   coating's application range. Corrective action: check spray gun
   setup against the coating manufacturer's chart; check booth ambient
   conditions before adjusting application technique.
