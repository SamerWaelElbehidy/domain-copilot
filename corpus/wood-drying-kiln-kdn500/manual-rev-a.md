# Wood Drying Kiln KDN-500 — Maintenance & Operations Manual (Rev. A)

## Overview & Specifications

The KDN-500 is a gas-fired batch dry kiln used at Dawlia Furniture Works'
Damietta facility to season rough-sawn hardwood lumber before machining.
Chamber capacity: 500 cubic feet. Maximum chamber temperature: 90C.
Heat source: natural gas burner with a forced-air recirculation system.
Chamber humidity is controlled by steam spray injection and vented
exhaust. A full drying cycle for 25mm hardwood stock runs 10-18 days
depending on species and target moisture content.

## Safety Prerequisites

1. Confirm the gas leak detector at the burner enclosure reads clear
   (no alarm) before every ignition sequence — never override a gas
   alarm to force a start.
2. Verify the chamber door interlock is functional: the burner must not
   be able to ignite while any chamber door is open.
3. Check that the exhaust vent damper is not obstructed before starting
   a cycle — a blocked vent can allow flue gas and carbon monoxide to
   accumulate in the chamber and burner room.
4. Do not open the chamber door within 2 hours of burner shutdown; the
   internal air temperature can still exceed 80C and the sudden
   temperature/humidity change can also warp the drying stack.
5. Confirm the fire suppression system's pressure gauge reads in the
   green zone and the sprinkler heads inside the chamber are unobstructed
   by stacked lumber before closing the doors for a cycle.
6. Never bypass the high-limit thermostat safety cutout — if it trips
   repeatedly, the cycle must be stopped and a certified technician
   called, not restarted.
7. Wear heat-resistant gloves when handling any chamber-adjacent metal
   fittings for at least 1 hour after shutdown.

## Installation & Setup

Install the KDN-500 on a reinforced concrete pad with clearance of at
least 1.5m on all sides for maintenance access and airflow. Gas supply
must be run by a licensed technician with a dedicated shutoff valve
outside the burner room. Route the exhaust flue vertically through the
roof with a minimum 4m rise to ensure adequate draft. Connect the
chamber's control system to the facility's fire alarm panel so a kiln
fire-suppression trip also triggers the building alarm.

## Operating Procedures

Load lumber stacks with uniform stickering (spacer strips) to allow even
airflow; uneven stickering is the most common cause of inconsistent
drying results. Set the target species profile on the control panel,
which sets the temperature/humidity ramp automatically. Confirm the
moisture-sensing pins are seated in sample boards before sealing the
chamber. Do not interrupt a cycle mid-ramp except for an emergency —
thermal shock from an unscheduled door opening can check (crack) the
lumber surface.

## Maintenance Schedule

Daily (during active cycles): check burner flame visually through the
sight glass, log chamber temperature/humidity against the target
profile. Weekly: clean humidity sensor probes, inspect door gasket
seals for gaps. Monthly: test the high-limit thermostat cutout function,
inspect the fire suppression sprinkler heads for dust/resin buildup.
Quarterly: full burner service and gas line inspection by a licensed
gas technician; calibrate the moisture-sensing pin circuit.

## Diagnostic & Troubleshooting

1. Symptom: burner fails to ignite, no error code shown. Likely cause:
   gas leak detector is in an unresolved alarm state, or a chamber door
   interlock is not fully engaged. Corrective action: confirm all
   chamber doors are fully closed and latched, check the gas detector
   panel for an active alarm before attempting a manual reset; do not
   force-start the burner via any manual override.
2. Symptom: uneven moisture content across a lumber stack at cycle end.
   Likely cause: inconsistent stickering or a partially blocked
   recirculation fan. Corrective action: inspect stickering pattern
   against the loading guide, inspect the recirculation fan blades for
   debris; re-run a shorter equalization cycle rather than re-drying
   from the start.
3. Symptom: chamber humidity will not reach the target set point.
   Likely cause: steam injection nozzle scaling/blockage, or a vent
   damper stuck partially open. Corrective action: inspect and
   descale the steam nozzle per the monthly checklist, verify the vent
   damper actuator moves freely through its full range.
4. Symptom: high-limit thermostat cutout trips repeatedly during a
   normal-profile cycle. Likely cause: a failing temperature sensor,
   or a stuck-open gas valve causing overfiring. Corrective action: do
   not restart the cycle; this is a certified-technician escalation per
   Safety Prerequisite §6, since it may indicate a gas valve fault.
